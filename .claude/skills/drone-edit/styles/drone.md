# Style: drone film

A travel / landscape film from drone footage, cut to the music. Parameters in `drone.json` (no limit on transitions,
no minimum shot length beyond the beat grid).

## Structure

Open on the strongest calm establishing shot under the title. Choose the music start so the drop lands roughly a third
to halfway into the film, and put the most striking shot on it. Cut faster at high energy. Slow motion (speed 0.5)
only on one to three hero moments. End on a calm pull-away under the end card.

## Editing doctrine

- **Open strong.** The first shot is the best establishing or reveal shot, long and calm (often slow-mo), under the
  title. The last shot pulls back or rises away, under the end card, into the fade.
- **Every cut on a beat**, bigger moments on bars; the music's drop gets a cut (the planner forces it) and is the one
  place for a `zoomin` or `smooth*` whip. Quiet parts: 3.5-5 s shots and soft `fade`s; full energy: 2-3 s, hard cuts.
- **The length is a ceiling, and shorter beats repeating.** Each clip at most twice, never the same source moment,
  never two similar frames of the same skyline back to back. If the footage runs out before the maximum, end the film
  early on a phrase. (User note on the first film: "some parts are repetitive, a shorter video would solve it.")
  Rough budget: about one 16:9 second per 2 s of usable footage, so 110 s of clips makes a ~45 s film.
- **Motion continuity.** Alternate or continue direction deliberately: a yaw right into a yaw right flows; a yaw right
  into a yaw left reads as a bounce. Hard-cut between different scales (wide to close, high to low).
- **Slow motion** on one to three hero moments (a wave breaking, a reveal over the buildings), not as filler. Only on
  clips shot at 50/60 fps (ANALYSIS.md says "slow-mo: yes"); build-cut refuses it on 24/30 fps clips.
- **Transitions sparingly.** Hard cuts carry a drone film. No wipes, no spins, no circleopen.
- **Never use** the first second of a clip (take-off lurch), the last ~0.7 s (stick release), a gimbal snap, or a shot
  where the sun flares across the lens unless it is the point.
